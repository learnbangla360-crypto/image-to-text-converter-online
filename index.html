Step-by-step: how to convert an image to text

1. Add your image

Drag a file into the box, paste an image from your clipboard, or tap the box to choose a file. A preview confirms you picked the right one. If the picture has a lot of extra background, use the Crop Image tool first so only the text area remains.

2. Choose the language

The reader works best when it knows the language. Pick the language of the words in the image, not the language of your device. A Spanish menu needs Spanish, even if you're reading this page in English.

3. Click Extract text

The first time, the page downloads the OCR engine and the language data, so the progress bar may sit on loading for a bit. Then it reads the image and fills the text box.

4. Check and edit

OCR isn't perfect. Skim the result against your image and fix odd characters, especially numbers, names and punctuation.

5. Copy or download

Click Copy text to paste it elsewhere, or Download TXT to save a plain text file. Click Reset / Start over for the next picture.

Expert tips for better results

  • Start with a sharp image. Blur is the top cause of bad OCR. Hold the camera steady and let it focus.
  • Shoot straight on. A page photographed at an angle bends the lines. Take the picture from directly above.
  • Fix the lighting. Shadows across a page confuse the reader. Even light works best.
  • Crop out the extras. Borders, photos and decorations can add junk characters.
  • Choose the right language. Using English on a French page gives you accent errors and wrong letters.
  • Mind the size. Tiny text benefits from a bigger source image. For oversized files, use the Resize Image tool, then try again.
  • Proofread numbers. A zero and the letter O, or a one and a lowercase L, are easy to mix up. Check them before you use the text.
  • Count what you got. The Word & Character Counter helps when the text has a length limit.

Privacy and security

When you click Extract text, your browser reads the image and runs OCR on your device. The image is not uploaded to a server, and the text appears only on this page.

One honest detail: the OCR engine (Tesseract.js, including its WebAssembly core) and the language data are downloaded from the jsDelivr CDN when you click Extract text. Only those library files are downloaded. Your image never leaves your device. Your browser may keep the language data in its cache so later scans start faster.

Still, treat extracted text with care. If an image shows passwords, ID numbers or private documents, handle the text as you would the original.

Troubleshooting

"No text found" message

The image may be too blurry, too dark or too small. Use a sharper, brighter version, crop to the text, and pick the right language.

The result is full of strange characters

Usually the wrong language is selected, or the image has a busy background. Switch the language and crop away decoration.

Words are merged or split

Low resolution causes this. Use a larger source image, or retake the photo closer to the text.

It's stuck on "loading"

The first run downloads the OCR files. Wait a bit on slow connections. If it fails, check your network and any blocker that stops jsDelivr, then click Extract text again.

My file won't open

Some formats, like HEIC from iPhones, don't open in every browser. Convert the photo with our HEIC to JPG Converter and try again.

Line breaks look wrong

Turn on "Join broken lines into paragraphs" to merge lines inside a paragraph, then click Extract text again. Turn it off to keep the original line breaks.

Handwriting isn't read correctly

OCR is built for printed text. Neat block capitals sometimes work, but cursive handwriting usually doesn't. Type those parts yourself.

Glossary

  • OCR: optical character recognition, the technology that reads letters from images.
  • Tesseract: an open-source OCR engine. See the Tesseract project.
  • Language data: a trained file that tells the engine what letters and words look like in a language.
  • DPI: dots per inch, a measure of image sharpness for scans.
  • Binarization: turning an image into black and white to separate text from background.
  • Confidence: the engine's own estimate of how sure it is about the text it read.

How accurate is the picture to text result?

It depends on the image. Clear, printed text on a plain background usually reads well. Blurry photos, low light, small type, unusual fonts and busy backgrounds cause mistakes. Always proofread the output, especially numbers, names and punctuation, before you rely on it.

Can it read handwriting?

Not reliably. The OCR engine is designed for printed text, so neat block letters might partly work, while cursive and messy handwriting usually comes out wrong. For handwritten notes, expect to retype much of the text yourself, or use a clearer, printed source.

Which languages does it support?

You can choose English, Spanish, French, German, Portuguese or Italian. Select the language of the text in your image, not your device language. If an image mixes languages, pick the main one and check the result carefully for accent and spelling errors.

Does it work offline?

Not on first use. The OCR engine and language data have to download from a CDN when you click Extract text, so you need a connection. Your browser may cache the language file, but you still need to be online to open the page.

Will it lose quality or change my image?

No. The tool only reads your image and never changes the original file. For reading, it works on a temporary copy that may be scaled up or down in memory. The text you get is a separate result that you can edit, copy or save.

Why is the extracted text wrong or empty?

Usually the image is blurry, dark, tiny or cropped badly, or the wrong language is selected. Try a sharper image, crop to just the text, choose the right language and retake photos straight on in even light. Handwriting and decorative fonts are common causes too.
